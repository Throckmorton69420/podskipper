## 1. Core AI Qwen3 4B: what closes PodSkipper

**His 30 Oct export.** #691 closed PodSkipper at part 4 of 6 (≈1,679 prompt tokens) with the note "writing classification · 1 tokens · engine had held 1808". Pass 53's fix read the longest part first (1,694 + answer = 1,808 positions), so this engine had *already* crossed 1,024 — **Pass 53's 1,024-position hypothesis is disproved.** The same part, after almost the same history, finished on 22 Oct. Jared Freid #954 (13 parts, 8 of them past 1,024 positions on a used engine) finished on the same build.

**All five sequential-engine closures** (22 Oct Tristan p3, 23 Oct #691 p2, 27 Oct #691 p3, 28 Oct Jared p8, 30 Oct #691 p4; none on an engine's first part): every one stopped between the first and the 16th answer token (the note's stage only moved at 1 and 16), on a part that wasn't the engine's first, with 5.27–5.55 GB still allowed by iOS (`os_proc_available_memory`). MetricKit (40 payloads, 18 Oct–29 Oct) has **no** crash diagnostic after 19 Oct and **no** foreground exit of any kind on the days of the closures — only background memory-pressure exits. PodSkipper was ended from outside, not by an exception it could see or that iOS reports as a crash. MiniCPM5 2B on the same engine and route never closed (≈70 comparable parts). Across exports: 0 closures in ~19 first parts, 5 in ~50 later parts (Fisher p ≈ 0.19 — "first part is safe" isn't established either).

**Where 1,024 comes from.** Apple's sequential engine (`coreai-models`, `CoreAISequentialEngine.selectPrefillStrategy`): up to `ModelConfig.chunkThreshold` (default **1,024**) new tokens are read in one forward pass, more in 512-token passes; each answer step re-binds `position_ids` of the whole length. Not a bug line — a width.

**Measured on the Mac** (`Tools/CoreAILab`, new `LAB_ORDER=longest` and `LAB_MEMLOG` — `MemorySampler` samples `phys_footprint` and `MTLDevice.currentAllocatedSize` every 10 ms with the part and step; the macOS Qwen3 4B bundle; #691 read the phone's way, 6 parts; `build/p54/run.sh`, traces `build/p54/mem/`):
- With Core AI's own widths the footprint crept part by part (≈1.0 → 2.7 GB), and in one run leapt **1.3 → 4.2 GB in 0.6 s at the second answer step of part 5** (Metal +0.74 GB at the same moment) — the phone's closure window; in another, Metal jumped +2.3 GB (≈ the model's weights) at the first part's answer.
- Read in 256-token steps (`COREAI_CHUNK_THRESHOLD=256`): footprint ≤ 1.28 GB.

| Setting (3 reads each, same session) | Peak footprint + graphics | Peak footprint | Time | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core AI default (1,024 / 512) | 8.35 / 9.30 / 10.92 GB | 2.60–4.55 GB | 34–41 s | identical |
| 256-token steps (as shipped now) | 6.70 / 6.70 / 6.70 GB | 1.27–1.28 GB | 34–37 s | identical |
| 256 + fresh-engine guard | 6.70 / 6.70 / 6.70 GB | 1.28 GB | 33–37 s | identical |

All 9 cut files are byte-identical (`md5 2cc7c63e…`): the width changes memory, not answers. Over the whole session 4 of 15 reads at 256 still showed one transient ~2.3 GB spike (Metal, or footprint 3.45 GB once, at an answer step of the engine's second part); after the guard made a fresh engine every later part stayed ≤ 1.0 GB. A forced fresh engine before every part (1,024 widths) brought the peak from 2.66 to 1.52 GB but cost 25 % more time, and once 31 s for the reload on the Mac. Core AI writes nothing to the system log about this (`log stream`: only a per-step shape-inference line), so what the spike is inside Apple's runtime isn't visible; its size is the model's weights.

**Mechanism (as far as can be established without iOS's report):** at prefill → answer transitions Apple's Core AI runtime makes transient multi-GB allocations (graphics and process memory), larger and more frequent with wider prefill passes and as an engine ages; on an 8 GB iPhone holding a 2.3 GB model a ~3 GB swing is what iOS ends a foreground app for without a crash report. Not proven on the phone: the phone's own per-step memory at a closure is the missing measurement, now recorded (below).

**Changes (`Services/CoreAIClassifierSession.swift`, `CoreAIAdJudge.swift`):**
- `CoreAIPrefill`: the sequential engine reads prompts in 256-token passes (`COREAI_CHUNK_THRESHOLD`, set for the bundle loaded and cleared for others; one token for decode-only ports as before).
- `CoreAIFootprint`: footprint and graphics memory measured; a fresh engine (old one released first) before a part when the last part's peak grew > 1,200 MB above where the engine settled after its first part.
- Removed: Pass 53's `CoreAIPositionLine` (fresh engine at a first 1,024 crossing) and the longest-part-first order — episode order again.
- The crash note now carries the footprint and graphics memory at every one of the first 32 answer steps ("PodSkipper was using N MB (graphics M MB) at the last step noted"); each part's memory peak is in Results › partReads ("memory peak N MB").
- Recovery unchanged (one automatic retry per episode, owed reads).

## 4. Real-episode evaluation without leakage (`Tools/DetectionLab/episode_eval.py`)

What it separates: an engine's **own** cuts (a model before the app's cut check; Apple Intelligence's detector), its **saved** (Reader-fused) cuts, the **Reader as shipped**, the **Reader from show-held-out folds**, and **no cuts**; per class (ads, plugs, other shows, intros, outros) heard s/h and stretches missed, show cut s/h, cuts wholly in the show, wrong kind, break edges; each engine only on episodes it read fresh (attempts that reused earlier parts excluded); his own corrections scored separately.

**Leakage and dependence found:**
- The shipped Reader (Pass 44) was trained on the labels of passes ≤ 44: **58 of the 71 labelled episodes**. Only 13 are unseen by it. Scoring it — or any model's *saved* cuts, which fuse it — on the 58 flatters it.
- Pass 53's fold Reader cuts were made with the 19 Oct detector binary (`build/p43-reader/lab-segments`), before Pass 46's detector changes. Rebuilt from today's source: `build/p54/lab-segments`.
- Models don't read episodes independently: in "focused" mode (every model read on the phone) they read only what the shipped Reader flagged, the opening/closing minutes and selling lines; a model's misses are partly the Reader's.
- The labels are Claude-labelled from transcripts. The only human-reviewed truth is his corrections: 29 on 4 episodes (all locks/confirms/adds/edits — no rejections, so they can't measure show cut).

**Results** (`build/p54/eval.md`; s/h = seconds per hour of audio; "own" vs a Reader that never saw any episode of that show):

## 5. Reader

- Standing without leakage: the Reader (held-out show, transcript only) hears less than every model's own cuts on the same episodes, but cuts more of the show than MLX Qwen3.5 4B (14.3 vs 8.3 s/h) and its Instruct build (13.8 vs 11.5). That show cut is the gap to close.
- **Detector precision sweep** (today's detector over Pass 53's show-held-out answers; folds 1–2 choose, fold 0 checks; `build/p54/sweep1.log`): `keepFloor` 0.3/0.4/0.5 and `joinFloor` 0.25/0.4 change nothing at all; `switchCost` 1 → 1.5 → 2 only adds heard time (tune 52.2/14.3 → 52.8/14.2; check 24.4/17.8 → 25.0/17.7). The show the Reader cuts is sentences it is confidently wrong about; no detector setting reaches it. Pass 53's architecture/data variants (external data, boundary weighting, context layer) each traded ranking gains for show cut.
- **Decision: no Reader or detector change shipped.** Shipped readers and defaults unchanged.
