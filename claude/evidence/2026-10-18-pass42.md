## 2. Changes (Pass 42 A, 6986e50)

- **`AudioEngine` feeds the node itself.** A serial queue reads and decodes one second at a time and keeps 20 s (7.7 MB at 48 kHz stereo) given to the node ahead of what it has played; the first half second is read before the node starts. A reading stall now has 20 s to pass before anything is heard. Every reschedule bumps a generation, so a flushed buffer's completion is ignored. The last buffer reports "played back", so the episode ends when its sound does.
- **`health()`** — asked by the player's tick only while it believes it is playing and no interruption is active:
  - *starved*: the node has nothing queued, isn't at the end of the file, and its clock is more than 0.25 s past the last frame it was given; reports where the sound stopped;
  - *clock stopped*: engine running and node playing, but the engine's render time hasn't moved (or is nil) for 1.5 s (`RenderClockWatch`).
- **Recovery** (`PlayerEngine.restartSound`): starved → play again from where the sound stopped (no jump back, no position lost); clock stopped → stop the engine and play from the position. At most **3 in 2 minutes** (`SoundRecoveryBudget`); a fourth pauses with the position kept and the next Play reschedules. Never when he paused, never during an interruption, never on healthy playback (healthy = audio queued or the clock moving). Speed, EQ, levelling, mono, route and session are untouched (the graph isn't rebuilt).
- **Position** never runs past the audio given, so a starved node no longer advances the scrubber, saved progress or Now Playing.
- **Diagnostics**: every trace entry's engine state now ends with "given to N s (X s ahead), slowest read N ms[, ran dry N×][, read failed: …]"; entries carry `heat` and `work` (e.g. "model test Bonsai-27B-mlx-1bit", "Find Ads"); a recovery writes one trace event and one background-log line with heat, the heavy work running and free memory ("Sound stopped while playing; started again at 56:40 · heat serious · during model test … · 0.8 GB free"). Nothing is logged per tick.

Unit (`Pass42AudioTests`, real engine in the simulator, 7 tests): healthy feed stays ahead and counts on; **a starved node (reading held by a test hook) is reported starved while `isRendering` is still true, the position stays where sound stopped, and playing from there resumes**; reaching the end is not starvation and stopping afterwards doesn't hang; pause/resume keeps the queue; clock watch; budget; work names. `ChapterPlaybackTests` still passes.

**What the phone has to show:** play an episode, start a heavy model test (the 27B, or a 4B at serious). Either the sound carries on (the 20 s lead covered the stall) or, if it stops, it comes back by itself within about a second and Diagnostics › Playback shows "sound ran out … starting again at …" with heat and the test named. If it pauses after three restarts, that line says so, and the "slowest read" and "read failed" figures say why.

## 3. Drag to choose (Pass 42 B)

Why Pass 41 didn't scroll on his phone (source + sim):

- The drag was a SwiftUI `DragGesture` on the **first row's tick**. A list recycles a cell once it scrolls out of sight; the gesture went with it, so a run ended about a screen after the list began to move. Pass 41's "33 rows on Downloaded" was that screen plus the steps before the cell left.
- The edge scroll stepped **one row index every 0.1 s** with `ScrollViewReader.scrollTo(…, anchor:)` — visible jumps, a speed fixed in rows not points, and the selection moved by index, not by what was under the finger.
- A resting finger sends no gesture updates, so nothing re-read what was under it.
- `scrollDisabled` while dragging and a SwiftUI layout frame for the edges were workarounds for the same design.

Now (`Views/CollectionKit.swift`): `SelectionPan` is a UIKit `UIPanGestureRecognizer` attached to the list with `UIGestureRecognizerRepresentable`. It receives a touch only if it starts in the tick column on a row being chosen (the list's own pan then waits for it; anywhere else the list scrolls as always). It lives as long as the list. The finger's screen position is kept while it rests. A `CADisplayLink` scrolls the list's own `UIScrollView` every frame while the finger is within 64 pt of the uncovered top or bottom (the scroll view's `adjustedContentInset`, which includes the bars, the actions bar and the mini player), after a 0.3 s rest, at 90–1,100 pt/s by depth squared, then chooses through the row under the finger. Rows are looked up from their reported screen frames; a finger over a bar chooses the last row that can be seen. Starting on a ticked row unticks; crossing back restores; one haptic per row, at most 20 a second. Pages keep the same API (`handle`, `SelectionRow`, `SelectionTick`, `.selectionDragArea`).

Simulator results (Xcode 27.1, iPhone 16 Pro simulator, iOS 27.0):

- **New lab `ResponsivenessLabUITests/testDragSelectScrollsOn`** (the simulator's own library; checks the viewport moved, the run is one unbroken block from its first row, the upward untick also scrolls, and scrolling beside the ticks changes nothing — by walking back up and reading every row's ticked state):
