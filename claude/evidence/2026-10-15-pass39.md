## 2. Cut check: the Reader overwrote granular model cuts (area 1)

- **Root cause (source + export):** `ModelCutCheck.verify` added a sure Reader cut back unless *one* model cut covered half of it. WMBD: Reader 2150–2385.6 (235 s); the model's Rocket Money, Hims, Underdog each covered 26–32 %, together 90 % → the whole Reader block went back in; `merge` joined same-kind (`ad`) cuts, swallowing Rocket Money and Hims, and left Underdog (`crossPromo`) overlapping inside it. Same mechanism on Theo (Quince + Chime → one 155-s Reader block) and Stavvy (Twisted Tea + AG1 → one block named Twisted Tea).
- **Fix (197539d):** union coverage decides. The model's cuts keep their edges and sponsors; the Reader fills the holes between them (extending the next read back over its lead-in, up to 90 s), the lead-in before the first (≤ 60 s, where models start late), and the end only where its lines sell. Where the model covered under half of a sure Reader stretch, the Reader's stretch stands around the model's cuts. Also: a Reader cut the model independently covers ≥ 25 % of counts at confidence ≥ 60 (Stavvy's post-roll); no two cuts overlap (`separate`); one read answered in pieces is checked as one (`joinSplitReads` — Cosentix had been dropped as two < 6-s pieces); "plug some dates" opens a plug round.

## 3. Prompt reuse and heat (area 3)

- **Redundant work found (source):** MLX re-read the rules and the episode header on every part (`reusedPromptTokens` always 0 for MLX; the held-to-the-format loop builds its own cache).
- **Lab (this Mac, Qwen3.5 4B 4-bit from his HF cache, Python mlx-lm, six real parts of WMBD and Stavvy's #202, greedy):** shared prefix ≈ 1,200 tokens of 1,900–3,300 per part. With the prefix cut to whole 256-token chunks (1,024) and fixed 256-token prefill chunks, **6/6 answers token-identical** to reading each part whole; prefill per part 30–50 % shorter (e.g. 12.2 → 8.7 s, 8.7 → 5.2 s, 7.5 → 3.8 s); prefix read once in 3.4 s. Unaligned (1,204-token prefix), 4/6 identical — the two differences were floating-point order, not content loss, and are why the alignment is used.
- **App (this pass):** `PromptPrefixCache` + `PrefixedLanguageModel` (a pass-through model whose new caches are copies of the prefix, so the library's constrained loop starts part-way in; public MLX API only); MLX prefill uses `.remainder` 256-token chunks; retries read whole. Per part the run now records heat, rest, tokens read/kept and speeds (`partReads`, exported in Results). **Not run on a phone or on the Mac through the Swift path** (MLX doesn't run in the simulator); compiled for simulator and device.
- **Thermal pacing evaluated, not extended:** his MLX 6-bit WMBD runs — 13 Oct starting nominal: read 92.7 tok/s; 15 Oct serious throughout with a 20-s rest before each of 4 parts: read 90.3 tok/s. The rests added ~80 s to a ~180-s read with no measurable speed gain in these two runs. No proactive (fair-state) pacing was added. Per-part records now let the next runs measure it properly.
- **Cooling countdown restored:** Activity bar shows "Waiting for iPhone to cool · 0:42 so far · carries on in 8 s" (or "Paused: iPhone is too hot · … carries on when it cools") in orange while waiting; the Activity steps say the same plus that waiting isn't counted as progress; time left stops counting down while waiting; "Spent 6 min · 1 min 20 s of it cooling". Unit-tested (`ThermalPacing` wait states); sim: not shown (simulator never gets hot).

## 4. Core AI Qwen3.5 4B / 6-bit Instruct (area 2)

- **Published conversion (HF API, 15 Oct):** `mlboydaisuke/qwen3.5-4B-CoreAI`, last modified 14 Sep, one bundle `gpu-pipelined-b2/qwen3_5_4b_decode_int8hu_block32_sym`, **5.77 GB** (int8). Its card: no iPhone bundle ("4B-class graphs exceed on-device GPU specialization and need AOT (h18p) compilation"); prefill runs as pipelined single-token steps ("prompt tok/s ≈ decode tok/s"); needs the zoo's engine patches.
- **Against his iPhone 16 Pro:** PodSkipper may use 6.11 GB (his Diagnostics) — a 5.77 GB weight file leaves no room for the cache, runtime and app. iOS caps pipelined Core AI at 1,024 tokens and the sequential engine at 2,047 (Pass 38), against ~3,300-token parts. And PodSkipper's work is reading: 12,000–19,000 prompt tokens per episode against 300–735 written. At decode-speed prefill (single-digit to ~20 tok/s for 4B on the phone, from his 8–9 tok/s MLX writing) a read would take 4–10× MLX's. h17p AOT compile of a 5.7 GB graph on this 17 GB Mac risks the hang seen with Gemma. **Not pursued — not technically reasonable on this phone.**
- **6-bit Instruct:** MLX's 6-bit weights would have to be dequantized and re-exported (the zoo has `mlx_to_hf_qwen3_5.py`), compounding two quantizations, into the same too-large, decode-speed-prefill graph. Same conclusion.
- **Fair comparison kept:** the working MLX models are unchanged; the prompt reuse above makes MLX cheaper, not different.

## 5. Gemma 4 E2B closed PodSkipper even with the compiled copy (area 2)

- **Export:** attempt at 01:29:54Z says the previous read closed the app at "part 1 of 12 · ~850 prompt tokens · CoreAIPipelinedEngine · **compiled for this iPhone** · word tables in memory". So the h17p compiled copy was in use — Pass 37/38's "install the compiled copy" was not sufficient.
- **MetricKit:** two crashes (diagnostic-1792026652, -1792027768; 21:10 and 21:29 local): `EXC_BREAKPOINT`/SIGTRAP — `_assertionFailure` in libswiftCore, called from CoreAIRuntime (system framework, stripped in the 27.0.1 device symbols, so Apple's frames can't be named), called from PodSkipper.
- **PodSkipper frames** (b457c86 rebuilt locally in a temporary worktree; its UUID 6DE0449D differs from the CI build's 51D3B869, so these are a close, not exact, match — consistent with Pass 38's): `EngineImpl.processChunkedInput(tokens:)` (CoreAIPipelinedEngine.swift:1971 — the one-token-at-a-time prompt loop, set by `COREAI_CHUNK_THRESHOLD=1` for decode-only bundles) ← `_encodeNextStepGPU` ← `CoreAIPipelinedEngine.generate`. So: prompt reading of part 1 (~850 tokens), with the compiled copy and tables in use — **not** the on-device compile Pass 38 blamed.
- **Change:** Gemma 4 E2B is now refused on iPhones without the publisher's build even with a compiled copy, with that reason (`CoreAIGemmaReadiness`); Pass38Tests updated. It stays offered on iPhone 17 Pro (h18p publisher build) — unverified there. A fix needs Apple's runtime (or symbols for it); not pursued blind.
- **Not run on the Mac** (the Pass 37 watchdog reset).
