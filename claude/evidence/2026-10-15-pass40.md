## 2. WMBD part 4: `GuidedGenerationError error 0` (export, source, lab)

- `error 0` is `GuidedGenerationError.incompleteOutput` (mlx-swift-lm `GuidedGenerationError.swift`): the held answer reached `maxTokens` before the JSON closed.
- **Cause.** Part 4 held the plug round (§1). The lean schema allows 8 parts, but MLX models write the held answer **indented**. In the Mac lab that costs 50 tokens a part (Qwen3.5 tokenizer) against 27 written on one line. Eight parts therefore needed ~400 of the 320 tokens allowed. Near the limit the loop's closing bias pushes the list shut; this time the answer still didn't close.
- **Then.** The retry read the whole 4,549-token prompt again, without the shared prefix, and wrote freely. It closed the primed list at once, `],"sponsor":""}`. That empty answer was kept as "no ads in this part". Its stats read "0 tok/s, wrote 0" because the stream ended before its summary. Prefix reuse, heat and retries didn't cause the failure; they only shaped what happened after it.
- **Prompt reuse checked through the app's Swift path** (Tools/MLXLab, Qwen3.5 4B 4-bit, `PromptPrefixCache` + the held loop with kv8 and 256-token `.remainder` prefill): **7/7 answers token-identical** to whole reads (Theo 4 parts, WMBD 3). Prefill per part was 30–55 % shorter (e.g. 10.4 → 6.8 s).
- **Theo part 4 on the phone** wrote 47 tokens on 8839e72, and its Plaid tag at 98:21 (~5,901 s) was missing. On b457c86 the same prompts found it (identical prompt totals). Not reproduced on the Mac (4/4 identical). Part answers weren't exported then; each part's record now includes the start of its answer. The Reader kept the cut, so nothing was heard.

**Built (8c6f0dc):**

- The held answer may be as long as the schema's parts need (`heldAnswerCap`: lean 496). Lab: answers that already fitted are byte-identical at 320 and 496 (5/5).
- An answer that still runs out of room is read again as two halves, each held to the format. Lab: halves complete under a forced 150-token cap.
- A free answer that names nothing, after the held one ran out of room, is **not** kept. That stretch stays with the Reader as unread.
- Retries and halves start from the shared prefix.
- What a failed answer wrote is kept, with its tokens and time. Wasted read/written tokens are counted.
- Free-text stats come from the stream itself.

**Measured and rejected (lab):**

- Holding the answer to one line with no spaces made Qwen3.5 name nothing in 2 of 4 Theo parts (115 against 506 answer tokens).
- One line with single spaces found every ad with 31 % fewer answer tokens but moved boundaries (Plaid 85–94 → 91–94, Manscaped 55–71 → 55–70).
- Neither is in the app (`Tools/MLXLab/Sources/mlx-lab/LabGrammar.swift`).
- 512- and 1,024-token prefill chunks were no faster than 256 (277/274 vs 281 tok/s) and change numerics.

## 3. What iOS tells an app about heat (Apple documentation)

- **Available in real time:** `ProcessInfo.thermalState` — nominal ("within normal limits"), fair ("slightly elevated"), serious ("high"), critical ("significantly impacting the performance of the system and the device needs to cool down") — and `thermalStateDidChangeNotification`. Also `isLowPowerModeEnabled` and its notification, plus `os_proc_available_memory()`. Apple's guidance in Xcode's "Responding to power notifications" by state:
  - fair: defer work whose results aren't needed now.
  - serious: reduce networking, location, screen updates and animations.
  - critical: "reduce or stop all work".

  An Apple engineer on the developer forums: reduce or defer background work at fair; reduce foreground effort at serious and critical.
- **Not available through public API:** temperatures, clock frequencies, GPU utilisation, throttling level, thermal pressure beyond the four states, the timing or hysteresis of state changes (undocumented), and per-process energy in real time.
- **What the app can measure itself, as proxies:**
  - each state change and its time (now `HeatLog`);
  - tokens per second per part, which is how throttling shows;
  - wall time spent waiting;
  - free memory at each part (now in `partReads`);
  - main-thread busy time.

  MetricKit adds daily CPU/GPU time, hang diagnostics and app responsiveness, but not in real time.
- **Needs Xcode or Instruments:** Power Profiler (CPU/GPU/display power), Metal System Trace (GPU timeline), Time Profiler, Hangs, the Thermal State track. Xcode's Devices window can force a heat state on a connected device for testing.

## 4. Heat policy: what changes the work, what doesn't (source, reasoning, unit)

- A read's work is fixed by the model and the transcript it must read. Every prompt token is one prefill position and every answer token one full pass over the weights. Pausing doesn't reduce that work; it moves it later. "Serious" means iOS has already slowed the chip. Slower clocks run at lower voltage and use less energy per token, which makes reading continuously at the pace iOS sets the efficient sustained pace. Sprinting and stopping is the opposite. The phone measurement below should confirm or refute this; it is a design reason, not a number from his phone.
- **What actually lowers a read's demand:**
  - fewer tokens: the shared prefix (−1,024 a part), no whole re-read on a retry, no answer run to its limit and thrown away;
  - fewer wasted passes: overflow fixed at its cause.

  These are built.
- **Considered and not built:**
  - smaller or larger chunks: no effect on total work, and on the Mac larger chunks were no faster;
  - shorter parts or less overlap: the same tokens, and coverage risk at the joins;
  - a smaller model or skipped transcript when hot: forbidden by his accuracy rule;
  - many short sleeps: rejected in his request;
  - kv8 off: changes answers;
  - batched fast-forward of forced JSON tokens: needs the library loop changed; in the lab 46 % of an indented answer is structural tokens. A candidate for later.
- **Built (8c6f0dc):**
  - **Policy "keep reading" (default).** No rest at nominal, fair or serious.
  - **Critical pauses** between parts and between chunks within a part (prefill progress and every 8 answer tokens). The read carries on after 10 s in a row out of critical, so it can't flap at the boundary. It gives up after 5 min with `TooHot`, and the Reader's cuts are saved.
  - **"Rest 20 s before each part"** is kept as a comparison choice in Diagnostics › Speed › Heat. Each run records `heatPolicy`.
  - **Records per run:**
    - `heatTimeline` (states with their times), `secondsByHeat`, `cooledSeconds`;
    - `wastedPromptTokens` / `wastedGeneratedTokens`;
    - `classifiedTokensPerMinute`: whole-eq tokens per wall minute, waits and retries included;
    - per part: heat at start → end, rest, tokens read / kept / written, speeds, seconds, free memory, the start of the answer, and "ran out of room" / "free text".
  - Heat changes go to the background log with how long the previous state lasted. Core AI reads record their waits and parts the same way.
- **Unit:** Pass40Tests:
  - serious doesn't wait;
  - the comparison policy rests 20 s;
  - critical resumes only after 10 s in a row out of critical, and a return to critical restarts the count;
  - the hold inside a part works only at critical;
  - the timeline;
  - throughput per minute;
  - an empty retry after an overflow isn't kept;
  - a failed answer's text is logged.

  Pass 29, 33 and 39 cooling tests now name the comparison policy.
- **Expected on his phone (projection, unverified):**
  - Theo would be ~175–190 s instead of 273 (active 173 s, no rests).
  - WMBD would avoid ~61 s of rests and the ~85 s spent on the overflow and its whole re-read.
  - Whether reading speed at serious holds without rests is what Diagnostics › Speed › Heat lets him measure: the same episode under both policies, back to back, both starting at serious.

## 5. Downloads slow to answer (sim, Instruments)

**Method.** The simulator's own library (14 shows, 5,817 episodes, 6 downloaded; not the demo). A debug-only `-LabStoreWrites` writes the store the way back-catalogue indexing does: 50 new episodes a save, about 2.5 saves a second, everything deleted afterwards. `ResponsivenessLabUITests` opens Library › Downloaded and leaves it alone for 30 s while Time Profiler records the app for 25 s. With `LAB_VISIT_TABS=1` it first opens every tab, as he uses the app.

| Main thread while Downloaded sits untouched | store quiet | store busy |
|---|---|---|
| before (8c6f0dc views), Library tab only | ~0 ms/s | **~75 ms/s** |
| before, every tab visited | ~0 ms/s | **~180 ms/s** |
| after, Library tab only | — | ~1 ms/s |
| after, every tab visited | ~0 ms/s | **~1 ms/s** |

**Where the time went (before, every tab visited, 25 s):**

- Library's `@Query` re-read every show on each save, under Downloaded: 335 ms of store reads, 364 ms of body.
- Up Next's queue `@Query`: 208 ms, plus its redraw.
- Downloaded re-read itself on every save: added in Pass 39 (`1fffab3`), with no relevance check, even while covered.
- New, Search and Settings redrew because their show `@Query`s were invalidated on every save.

`_printChanges` showed `@Query` redraws its view on every save even when its results don't change. A hidden New page redrew 178 times in 40 s, and Up Next 89.

**Fixed (Pass 40 B):**

- **Library:** shows are read when a show is added or removed, not on every save. A show's own fields still update through observation.
- **Downloaded, Saved, Latest, Recently Played:** re-read only for a save that can change the list, decided from the saved identifiers alone (`StoreChanges`):
  - one of its episodes changed or deleted;
  - an episode changed that now belongs on it;
  - for Latest, any new episode;
  - an episode not loaded on the main context, which can't be checked.

  Reads are coalesced, and are deferred while the page is covered, until it shows again.
- **Up Next and New/Search:** results come from `LiveQuery`, a zero-size view holding the `@Query`. It is present only while the tab shows. It passes results up only when which items, or their order, change.
- **Settings:** shows are read when OPML export is tapped.
- A kept list never draws a deleted model (`isLive`).

**Verified:**

- **Unit:** `StoreChanges` reports what saves touched (show added or removed vs. episode added or starred).
- **Sim UI:** `testPass40ListsFollowChanges` covers four behaviours:
  - Remove Download takes the row off Downloaded at once;
  - Play Next in Latest Episodes appears in Up Next when that tab opens;
  - Library lists its shows;
  - New shows its shelves.

  Screens are in `build/shots-p40a`.

**Not established:**

- **Which saves his phone makes while he browses.** Back-catalogue indexing, feed refresh on opening, iCloud and once-a-minute playback writes are the sources in code. The lab reproduces the mechanism, not his exact load.
- **The XCUITest tap timings** (open ~2.6 s, back ~1.9 s, before and after) are dominated by XCUITest's own waits and accessibility snapshots. They didn't move and don't measure the app.
- **Phone:** the acceptance check is his.

## 6. Core AI: Qwen3.5 4B and alternatives (repos, model cards, his exports)

**Sources read:**

- apple/coreai-models @9db7476: the `models/` catalog, the qwen3 recipes, `export/externalize.py`.
- john-rocky/coreai-model-zoo @567da99:
  - `models/qwen3.5`, `models/kev-4b`, `models/kev-0.8b`;
  - `knowledge/coreai-vs-mlx-speed.md`, `aot-and-specialization.md`, `pipelined-engine.md`, `apus-decision-v1-4b-port.md`, `qwen3.8-27b-port.md`;
  - `BENCHMARKS.md`, `conversion/export_qwen3_5_*`.
- The model card huggingface.co/mlboydaisuke/qwen3.5-4B-CoreAI.
- His model tests and episode runs.

**What the recipes establish about Qwen3.5 (Gated DeltaNet hybrid) on Core AI:**

1. **int8 is the floor.**
   - int4, as k-means g32, g8 with int8 rescue, or linear per-block-32, fails the oracle gate on 0.8B and on 2B: 10/16, and the HF-seeded decode step.
   - On Kev-4B, int8 over every linear failed the author's precision bar (0.060 against the bar; partial fp16 sets also fail).
   - The published 4B bundle is int8 (per-block-32, int8 head), **~5.4 GB**. APUS-Decision-4B is int8hu at 5.77 GB.
   - His iPhone 16 Pro allows PodSkipper **6.2 GB** (Diagnostics), before the KV, GDN states, specialization, runtime and app.

   Apple's own 4B iOS recipe (Qwen3 4B, dense) is mixed 4/8-bit palettized at 4.89 bits per weight. That recipe doesn't apply to the hybrid, by the zoo's gates. 6-bit palettization (Apple ships it for Qwen3 1.7B) is untested on Qwen3.5 anywhere we found. The zoo measured the 256-entry int8 LUT as slow on the GPU delegate.
2. **Prompt reading runs at decode speed.** The GDN recurrence lowers only loop-free at query length 1, so the pipelined engine reads prompts one token per step: "prompt tok/s ≈ decode tok/s".
   - Measured decode for the 4B int8 bundle: **10.8 tok/s on iPhone 17 Pro** (DeviceMark). There is no A18 Pro row anywhere.
   - PodSkipper reads 9,000–19,000 prompt tokens an episode. At ~10 tok/s that is **15–30 min**, against MLX's 3–6 min (88–117 tok/s reading on his phone).
   - Chunked prefill exists only as custom graphs. A q16 unrolled companion on 0.8B made prefill 2× faster (147 tok/s on iPhone 17 Pro). The chunked GDN scan overflows fp16 from S = 32 (qwen3.8-27b port).
   - Kev's 128-token prefill uses an fp32 Metal kernel, but its 4B release is **fp16 8.41 GB, Mac-only**, and its earlier iPhone 18 Pro asset crashed on load.
   - The zoo's own summary: "chunkwise-parallel GDN prefill is the open fix".
3. **4B graphs can't be specialized on the phone.** They must ship as an `.aimodelc` compiled ahead of time for the device class: `h17p` for his `iPhone17,1`, while the publisher builds `h18p`. A 4B on-device compile ran out of disk on an iPhone 17 Pro.
4. **This Mac can't produce one safely.** 16 GB RAM, 13 GB free disk: a 4B fp16 source is ~8.5 GB. A Mac AOT compile of a 1.8 GB graph used ~4 GB of host RSS. Gemma inference has already reset this Mac once.
5. **No logits on the fast path.** The pipelined engine doesn't expose logits, so constrained answers need the sequential engine with CPU-held answers, as PodSkipper does for Qwen3 4B.

**Verdict.** A Core AI Qwen3.5 4B would hold about 5.4 GB of int8 weights on a 6.2 GB budget, and would read prompts about 9× slower than the MLX model now in use. Both limits come from the architecture and today's Core AI lowering, not from one conversion. What would change it:

- a QAT int4 Qwen3.5 checkpoint, so 4-bit survives;
- chunkwise-parallel GDN prefill in Core AI, or a Kev-style kernel that fits in memory;
- an h17p AOT build made on a machine with more memory.

None is available today. **Not pursued; no download or compile started.**

## 6a. Kev-0.8B as an ad classifier (lab, 15 Oct, after §6)

**Method.**
- **Model:** the author's own code (github.com/jaredpalmer/kev @ `kev-1.0`) and checkpoint (`jaredpalmer/kev-0.8b@v1.0` on Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base `dc7cdfe`), run on its MLX backend on this Mac's GPU. Its probabilities matched the author's CPU fp32 path within 0.003 on the smoke questions; CPU took 15–20 s a question, MLX 0.08 s. No Core AI; nothing 4B.
- **Input:** each ~20 s chunk of the three hand-labelled episodes (WMBD, Stavvy's #202, Theo #690; labels from `Pass39Tests.testExportReplayOnHisThreeEpisodes`; transcripts from `build/phone-1015/…1792032802.json`) is the "passage". It goes in with the podcast, episode, 60 s before and 40 s after as the state, plus two yes/no questions: ad or sponsor read (including host reads and codes)? plug (show, other show, tour dates, merch, Patreon, guest's project)?
- **Scoring:** chunks over a threshold become cuts, scored like Pass 39 (ad seconds heard / show seconds cut).
- **Comparison:** MLX Qwen3.5 4B 6-bit's cuts from the same export.
- **Where things are:** script `Tools/DetectionLab/kev_eval.py`; every chunk's probabilities in `build/kev-lab/kev-run1.json` (git-ignored).

| Episode | MLX 6-bit, saved: heard / show cut | Kev, ad or plug ≥ 0.5 | Kev, ad or plug ≥ 0.6 | Kev, ad only ≥ 0.7 | Kev ≥ 0.5 inside the Reader's stretches only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WMBD | 169 / 10 s | 28 / **1,022 s** | 140 / 480 s | 369 / 0 s | 110 / 83 s (Reader alone 155 / 10) |
| Stavvy's #202 | 98 / 41 s | 85 / **909 s** | 243 / 526 s | 376 / 74 s | 85 / 292 s (Reader alone 20 / 14) |
| Theo #690 | 31 / 0 s | 77 / **483 s** | 252 / 221 s | 350 / 0 s | 77 / 96 s (Reader alone 23 / 1) |

- **Separation is real but not enough.** Chunk-level AUC is 0.93 (57 ad/plug chunks against 761 show chunks). Ad chunks score a median 0.56–0.68 and show chunks 0.32–0.40. With ads this rare, every threshold either cuts minutes of show or misses most ads. No threshold comes near MLX's balance, even inside the Reader's stretches: there it is worse than the Reader alone on all three episodes.
- **Speed (Mac M1 Pro GPU):** 158k–213k prompt tokens an episode in 120–170 s, every chunk with its context, so ~1,250 tok/s. Not measured on a phone.
- **Verdict:** zero-shot Kev-0.8B is **not worth a phone trial** for ad finding, and is not added to the app.
- **The only route that could change this:** fine-tuning from the released checkpoint on his labelled shows. The author's `--init_from` path is reported to keep general skill while learning a domain (0.33 → 0.88 on another user's support data), but it needs a rented GPU and a labelled set, and it is Batch 4 work, not built. Qwen3.5 4B on Core AI stays not practical (§6).
