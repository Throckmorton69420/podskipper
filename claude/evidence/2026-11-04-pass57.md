## 3. Method: compare at the same operating point (`Tools/ReaderData/frontier.py`)

Pass 53/56 compared each variant wherever it landed. A reader's sentence answers can be moved along one curve by a show bias δ (the show's logit + δ; ships as one number added to the head's `out.bias[0]`), so each held-out answer set is cut by the app's detector at δ = −1 … 2.5 and scored as a curve of ads heard vs show cut, with 95 % bootstrap intervals over episodes (paired against a base). Detector: `build/p57/lab-segments` built from HEAD. (`build/p56/lab-segments` was built during the Pass 56 experiment with the reader-edge rule on — it reproduces Pass 56's "baseline + reader-edge rule" row, not the app; don't use it.)

Noise floor: Pass 53's base and Pass 56's "older" are the same recipe (older adds ~10 agreement sentences — effectively a reseed). On the frontier they differ by up to ~2–3 s/h of show cut at matched heard, and at the sentence level (seconds-weighted, detector-free) by 14.8 vs 9.6 s/h show at 40 s/h heard. Any single-run difference smaller than that is not evidence.

**Previous variants on the frontier (show-held-out, 71 episodes, HEAD detector, policy 1 labels):** focal loss and equal class weights are dominated by the baseline; context layer (CTX 2), edge weighting (EDGE_W 3) and SponsorBlock data (EXT_W 0.5) all lie inside the base/older band (e.g. at ≈ 44 s/h heard: base 13.8, older 11.4, edge 11.2, ext 12.4, ctx 13.2 s/h show cut). Pass 53's "better ranking, more show cut" was an operating-point effect; none of them is a better curve either.

## 4. Experiments that failed, and why

| Experiment | Result (show-held-out) | Cause found |
|---|---|---|
| Larger encoder: all-MiniLM-L12-v2 (33 M, 384 wide) | fold 0: 32.7 heard / 13.2 show at δ 0, but at matched heard far more show (≈ 24 heard ↔ 38 show) and 30 kinds wrong vs 13; stopped after fold 0 | underfits under the Reader's recipe: final training loss 0.22 vs ELECTRA-small's 0.02–0.05 |
| Larger encoder: MiniLM-L12-H384 (Microsoft) | stopped at step 400 (loss 0.46 vs 0.19) | same; ELECTRA-small already fits its training shows almost perfectly — the limit is generalising to new shows, not capacity |
| Shorter training (1.5, 2 epochs; 3 ships) | both dominated (e.g. 1.5 ep at δ 1: 49.1 / 13.1 vs base 44.4 / 13.3) | 3 epochs is better; shorter training underfits more than it regularises |
| Boundary refiner stacked on held-out answers (`refiner.py`: neighbours' answers ±10, pauses, position, hand-off / "back to" / offer / plug / preview / credit phrases; linear and gradient-boosted) | worse at every δ (linear δ 1: 42.6 / 21.5; GBM 42.7 / 18.3 vs 44.8 / 12.1 for the same two readers alone); sentence AUC 0.981–0.982 vs 0.991 | the folds' readers are calibrated differently (each trained on other shows), so a second stage fitted on two folds' answers misreads the third's; the detector is not the cause (it beats naive decoding of the same answers: argmax runs 48.1 / 20.6 vs detector 41.5 / 15.7) |
| Tail weight averaging (`SWA=0.3` in `tagger.py`: the weights averaged over the last 30 % of steps, every 25), judged by a rule written first (`build/p57/ship-rule-2.txt`) against its exact twin (same seed, data, code) | seed 13: sentence level 19.8 vs 18.9 s/h show at 40 heard (worse); frontier with gaps δ 1: 43.9 / 11.5 vs 42.7 / 10.5 (more show at matched heard); fewer kinds wrong (28 vs 34). Rule needs both seeds better → failed; seed 17 stopped | averaging along a linearly decayed tail changes little; the variance between seeds is in where training goes, not noise at its end |
| Shipped readers at another bias (test episodes) | δ +0.5: heard +3.3 (0.0…+8.2), show −1.0 (−2.6…0.0); δ −0.5 the reverse | a pure trade; heard + 2 × show is worse either way — operating point kept |

## 5. Teachers (`Tools/ReaderData/teacher_annotate.py`, `silver.py`)

Teacher reads are whole-episode (no focus ranges), through the app's MLX path (`Tools/MLXLab`, rebuilt; `LabJudge` now writes every part's raw answer, label, confidence, funny flag and reason to `LAB_DETAIL`). Identity per read: model folder + snapshot, SHA-256 of config/index/tokenizer/template, profile, budget, cap, lab source revision. Teacher A: MLX Qwen3.5 4B 4-bit (mlx-community, `p-listener`, the app's profile for it). ~150 s per audio hour on this M1 Pro.

**Inference vs quality, kept apart.** Inference: every part answered within the cap and parsed (first 5 episodes: 43 parts, 0 out of room, 0 unparsable, 27 empty "nothing here" answers); the model's own `confidence` was 80 on every part it named — it carries no calibration, so a teacher's trust comes from agreement with other evidence, never from that number. Quality on all 11 test episodes (label policy 2, wordless gaps as EITHER): it lets **52.3 s/h** of ads through (paired vs the shipped Reader +38.9, +21.5…+58.6), misses 21 of 80 breaks (Reader 7) and cuts 7.3 s/h of show (+2.6, −2.4…+8.0: not different; 9.3 vs 11.2 without the gap rule). Reading the whole episode is worse than the app's focused reading for this model (its own focused reads on his phone: 36.2 / 3.8 on 8 test episodes).

**How teacher reads become training sentences** (`silver.py`, `TEACHER=<name>` in `reader_distill.py`): only for episodes no one labelled; a teacher's part (a kind the app skips) gives its inner sentences that kind — weight 1 when another family's read on his phone cut the same stretch as the same kind or a selling / plug word is inside, else 0.5 — and never its first or last sentence; the show is only taken more than 45 s from every teacher part, every cut any engine made on his phone, and every selling or plug word; his reviews override. Disagreements are therefore left out of training rather than averaged.

**Distillation run** (rule written first: `build/p57/ship-rule-3.txt`). The teacher read 48 unlabelled episodes (≤ 6 per show, 57.6 h) plus all 11 test episodes; the rules kept 68,293 show sentences and 3,763 promo sentences (3,268 backed), every part edge left out. Seed 13 with these sentences (`TEACHER=q35-4b LAB_OLDER=1`; fold 0 trained on 45 labelled + 32 teacher-read episodes) against its exact twin `base13`:

| | sentence AUC / AP | show at 40 heard (sentence level) | through the detector, gaps as EITHER, matched heard |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain (`base13`) | 0.9875 / 0.940 | 18.9 | δ 1: 42.7 / 10.5 · δ 0.5: 40.5 / 11.5 |
| + teacher sentences (`silver13`) | **0.9923 / 0.956** | **7.4** | δ 2.5: 42.4 / 10.6 · δ 2: 40.1 / 11.6 |

The teacher sentences make the Reader rank sentences much better (and shift its answers toward "promo", so it needs δ ≈ 2.5 for the same heard), but **through the app's detector the curve is the same as the twin's**. Retuning the detector (switch cost, keep and join floors, δ; chosen on two folds, checked on the third) didn't release it: 40.0 / 12.2 (heard + 2 × show 64.4) vs the twin at the app's defaults 38.3 / 12.3 (63.0); for the twin itself the cross-fitted choice was worse than the defaults (67.4), so the knobs overfit folds. Rule 3 needs a better curve → **failed; not shipped**; the second seed was stopped (moot). The detector's run and edge rules already remove the isolated sentence errors that dominate the sentence-level number; what is left at the cut level is where breaks start and end, which these sentences (edges dropped by design) don't teach. A second teacher (Instruct 6-bit) was not run (GPU time went to the Reader runs). Leakage note: the show sentences exclude zones around cuts made on his phone, which include the shipped Reader's (trained on every show); dropping those zones changes 1,865 of 68,293 show sentences — small, and moot after the failure.

## 6. Seed variability and ensemble size (show-held-out folds)

Four plain readers of the shipped recipe (seeds 7 ×2 — Pass 53 base and Pass 56 "older" — 13 and 17, this pass), sentence level (seconds-weighted, detector-free), show cut at 40 s/h heard: **9.6, 14.8, 16.6, 18.9** (mean 15.0, sd ≈ 3.9). Pairs: 11.3 and 15.3. All four averaged: 11.6. Through the detector (δ 1): singles 42.4–44.4 heard / 12.5–16.3 show; pairs 44.8 / 12.1 and 42.8 / 14.3; four 43.6 / 13.3 (with gaps: 7.9, 10.1, 9.0).

So averaging more readers makes the result more predictable (four beats the average single and the average pair) but not better than the best pair. An earlier five-reader average (base, older, edge, ext, ctx: 45.2 / 9.3 at δ 1.5) owed its gain to which readers were in it. **Training variance, not the recipe's mean, is the Reader's biggest uncertainty**: the shipped pair is one draw, and its standing on shows it has never met is unknown.

## 11. What a Reader improvement now has to show

1. A better **curve through the detector** (frontier with `--gaps`), not a better sentence ranking — two candidates this pass (tail averaging, teacher sentences) won at the sentence level and tied or lost at the cuts.
2. Beyond **seed noise**: four plain seeds span 9.6–18.9 s/h at the sentence level and ~2–4 s/h at the cuts; compare with paired twins (same seed) or several seeds.
3. On **episodes no reader saw** (the 11 test episodes, and his next export's new episodes once labelled under policy 2), by a rule written before the result.
4. The lever this pass points at: where breaks start and end (most remaining show cut touches a break), and his own corrections on Reader-unseen episodes.
