## 1. "Reaching Out": half an hour of "plugs" (export + lab)

- **What the export shows.** Two library entries for This American Life's "Reaching Out" — guid `46233 at https://www.thisamericanlife.org` and `fc9974a4-…` — with **the same audio address**. The second carried selfPromo 0:00–31:01 and 37:49–1:01:51 and two "ads" 31:34–35:10 and 35:35–37:49, every one stamped "the same recording plays in another episode or elsewhere in this one" (stage *fingerprint*). Its transcript at those times is the story (Sarah Cavedo's series), Ira's Life Partners plug (0:00–1:22, 1:00:12–1:01:17), a Wise spot and the credits. `detectorVersion` 44: it was the Pass 44 Reader, not the old one Pass 45 blamed.
- **Mechanism (source).** `SegmentDetector.withProduced`: (1) a repeat that overlaps more than half of an existing cut *widened that cut to the repeat's edges*, however long the repeat — the Reader's 97-s Life Partners plug became 31 minutes; (2) a repeat from another episode that the Reader read as conversation was still cut, by its place: an **ad** whenever it was 20 s or more in the middle of the episode — two acts of the story. Fingerprinting compares each episode with the show's last two: the duplicate's whole hour "played in another episode".
- **Reproduced on this Mac (lab).** The episode downloaded (`build/lab/tal899.mp3`, 64 min with this download's inserted ads) and fingerprinted against an identical copy: 22 "across episodes" runs of 12–757 s covering the hour, broken only at pauses. HEAD's detector (`lab-segments-p46base`, shipped Reader, `LAB_PRODUCED=1`) cut 0:00–5:02 as a plug, 5:02–18:59 as an intro and 14 "ads" through to 51:31, then 51:33–1:04:10 as a plug — the phone's failure, worse. A length cap alone could not have caught it: most runs are 12–220 s.
- **Fix (`AdPrints.withoutSharedProgram`, `SegmentDetector.withProduced`, `plausible`).**
  - Automatic across-episode evidence (repeats, and matches the cross-show library learned by itself) is set aside when it covers a third of the episode or ten minutes, whichever is more — the episode shares its program (a duplicate, a rerun, a best-of). Any single run over 10 minutes is never one recording. Within-episode repeats, the own library entries and the Not an Ad negatives stay. Applied in `detectionEvidence`, so cached evidence is filtered too.
  - A repeat widens a cut it doesn't mostly cover by at most 150 s.
  - A repeat the Reader reads as the show is the show; only an opening (≤ 120 s) or closing (≤ 360 s) is named by its place. A piece running on from a recording already cut still joins it.
  - Last check: no automatic cut longer than its kind can be (ad 12 min, plug 10, promo/intro 5, outro 8; stitched-in stretches exempt). The longest genuine cuts: a 463-s break of four spots, 460 s of plugs, a 326-s closing song.
- **After (lab, same download).** Plug 0:00–1:19, IXL spot 1:21–1:34, the two breaks (SNHU, Mint Mobile ×2, Progressive, Carvana), Futuro+ plug, credits, Life Partners plug, sign-off, the post-roll — and the story kept.
- **Library hygiene:** the cross-show library never learned the 216-s story stretch (it refuses recordings over 150 s); a 134-s one may have been learned from the duplicate. It would only match the same story audio, and the coverage rule sets it aside there.

## 4. Does the Reader really learn? (source + export)

- **The export:** 958 model-read sentences from three episodes, **0 from the corrections**, two heads kept on 22 Oct (lab-sentence loss 0.088 → 0.079 → 0.079), the newest rolled back (23 Oct 03:18 UTC).
- **Circular supervision — confirmed (source).** `ProcessingPipeline.noteCheck` taught the learner `checked.cuts`, the output of `ModelCutCheck`, which contains stretches "kept from PodSkipper's reader: the model didn't mention this stretch" and model edges "moved to where PodSkipper's reader heard the ad"; `otherTeachers` read every earlier attempt's `savedCuts`, the same mixture. On #691 the Tulsa King spot at 0:00 was Reader-only in both the Qwen3 4B and the MiniCPM5 save — to the learner, two families agreeing. The Reader was taught its own output; so was every edge it moved. Also: Core AI Qwen3 4B and MLX Qwen3.5 4B are one family, so MiniCPM5 was the only real second vote, and two Qwen reads counted as two votes for a kind.
- **The corrections never taught it.** 24 corrections (Whiskey Ginger 14, Pontoon Hound 9, YN Airlines 1, Kam Patterson 1 move) predate the learner (7 Oct); gold examples are made only when a correction changes. And stored examples carry the features of the readers that made them, with nothing checking that after Pass 44 replaced both readers.
- **Fix (`OnDeviceReaderLearner` policy 46, `ProcessingPipeline`):**
  - A model votes only with what it proposed itself, where its check kept it (`independent(proposed:kept:)` — proposals ∩ saves, its own kind). The Reader's additions and moved edges are not its.
  - One vote a family (first of the family: this read, then the newest).
  - Nothing any read or the Reader marked is taught as the show (`claimed`, 30 s clearance).
  - Model-read labels from policy 43 are dropped once; the head learned only from them is **set aside, kept on disk** (`UserTaggerHead-set-aside.bin`), and the Reader is as shipped until a head from checked evidence passes the same gates (lab sentences, held-out corrections, held-out agreement).
  - Examples stamped with the readers that made them; stale ones go.
  - `rebuild` (once per policy and pair of readers, from `maintain`): every episode with corrections becomes gold; every episode two families read, from the attempt log's proposals and saves, becomes agreement silver; one training afterwards.
  - What it learned from: `Status.evidence` (per episode: title, show, teacher — "your corrections", "models agreed: minicpm + qwen", "ad-free download" — sentences by kind), in Settings › Ad Skipping › Learned on This iPhone ("What It Learned From") and in the Diagnostics export (`readerLearning.evidence`); each history line says what it learned from.
- **T:** `Pass46LearnerTests` (5); `Pass43RecoveryTests` updated (a model's vote is now its proposal where kept).
- **Whole-Reader retraining: not done, on purpose.** Since Pass 44's 58 labelled episodes and Pass 45's #691 there is one new episode in the library (Stavvy's Bonus #203) and two more MLX reads; nothing else new and independently labelled. Pass 44's three-fold held-out spread between folds was larger than one episode can move. The levers this pass are the evidence the on-phone learner gets (the 24 corrections, for the first time, and agreement that is actually independent) and the detector stages above.

## 5. Qwen3 4B closures (export + source + lab)

- **The export:** closure 23 Oct 03:09–03:10 UTC, #691 part 2 of 6, "about 1,694 prompt tokens read · writing classification · 1 tokens", sequential engine, on screen, iOS allowing 5,373 MB more. The per-part checkpoint worked: the retry kept part 1 ("answered before the app closed, kept") and finished (109 s). The 22 Oct closure: #960 part 3 of 11, ~1,707 tokens, first answer token.
- **No report from iOS for either.** MetricKit in this export: the newest diagnostic payload was received ~17:50 UTC on 22 Oct (a disk-write and two CPU exceptions); no crash diagnostic for 22 Oct 06:59 even 21 hours later. Daily payloads up to 21 Oct show **no abnormal foreground exits at all** — including 19 Oct, with 22 closures counted — and background memory-pressure exits rising 3 (19 Oct), 7 (20 Oct), 10 (21 Oct). The 22–23 Oct daily isn't in this export. A closure that leaves no report is a jetsam kill or a GPU-side kill; iOS's daily exit counts in the next export a day after a closure say which.
- **Older reports read for this pass:** 16 Oct scene-update watchdog (main thread deep in SwiftUI/UIKit layout while backgrounded at serious — the Pass 41-era list redraws, since reworked); 14 Oct scene-update (30 s); two "failed to terminate gracefully" (19 Oct: a BackgroundTasks call; 15 Oct). None from the sequential engine.
- **Ruled out (source + export):** a window overflow (the same 1,694-token part 2 was read with 424 tokens kept on 22 Oct and finished; the plan keeps prompt + 170-token answer + 24 under 2,047); a race between steps (CoreAIKit's sequential iterator does all its work inside `next()`; breaking after one output leaves nothing running); the read watchdog (240 s, no retry into the same session); the in-flight note (UserDefaults). Prefills over 1,024 new tokens are already 512-token chunks.
- **What the data separates:** on the sequential engine, of the parts read to the end or closed on, the five of ~1,700 tokens or more closed it twice; the ~30 smaller ones never (Fisher p ≈ 0.02). MiniCPM5's parts (≤ 1,300) never closed it.
- **Change (`ModelPromptPlan.promptCeiling`, Core AI Qwen3 > 2.5 B only):** each part's whole prompt stays under 1,536 tokens (`windowRoom`); the plan says so on the model's page. Root cause still **not established**; this keeps parts below every closure seen. Quality on the real build: see §6.
